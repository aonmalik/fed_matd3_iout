## 1. Statistical protocol (2.1)
10 independent training seeds (0-9), 500 episodes, 20 matched evaluation scenarios; manifest, YAML, validator, design report, runtime estimates and docs updated consistently (`PROTOCOL_CHANGELOG.md`). Design report (computed, not hard-coded): exact Wilcoxon minimum p = 2^-9 = 0.00195; Holm over 10 comparisons *attainable* (needs >= 9 seeds). Attainable is not guaranteed: it needs all paired differences to share a sign and says nothing about power for realistic effects. Seeds are the unit; policies that are nearly identical give constant differences, which return NaN, not a fabricated p. Cost (measured only for `s3a_fedmatd3`, 10.5-12.2 s/episode): ~1.6-1.7 h per 500-episode run, ~180-190 h sequential for 110 runs; other methods untimed.

## 2. Retry / back-off
Deterministic, bounded: after the k-th consecutive REJECTED/EXPIRED update the client waits min(480, 120*2^(k-1)) s (base = agg_timeout_s, cap = 4x; a-priori, not tuned; no jitter). Reset by a commit or a newer global install. It only *defers* the next upload: lifecycle, reference and progress counters are untouched (reference never advances on rejection; tests assert bit-equality). No hard suspension, on purpose (it could exclude a client permanently if no aggregation happens). Tracked per client and in `summary()`: retry attempts, deferred updates/steps, rejected bytes, expired bytes, wasted uplink fraction, back-off seconds. 12 deterministic tests (`tests/test_retry_backoff.py`) incl. analytic bound on attempts, healthy client unaffected, timeout expiry, statistics returned exactly once, protocol-2.0 config has no retry.
**Measured impact (persistent rejection for 600 s, test-scale model, same fault):** uploads 26 -> 2, uplink bytes x0.077, transmit airtime/energy x0.077. **Latency cost (fault heals at t=100 s):** first commit at +3 s without back-off vs +26 s with (+23 s; bounded by the current back-off delay <= 480 s). **Real-scale energy:** one full upload costs ~24.7 s of AUV transmit airtime (77 kB model, probe) = ~742 J at the simulator's assumed 30 W `p_tx_elec_w` (uncalibrated assumption), so each avoided repeated upload saves that much; the probe itself had 0 rejections, so the saving is a conditional, not an observed, benefit. Back-off applies identically to all five federated methods.
**Bug found while testing:** `install_global` with blend = 1 computed `1*theta + 0*local`, turning a non-finite local parameter into NaN in the installed actor (silent corruption). Fixed as an exact replacement; identical for finite values.

## 3. Semantic aggregation (formula unchanged)
Diagnostics (`validation/aggregation_diagnostics.py`, `docs/aggregation_diagnostics.json`, per-run in `run_one`): per-client relevance and weights, participants per round, single-update fraction, log-variance shares of n / staleness / relevance, counterfactual (relevance factor = 1) weight TV and relative parameter-update change (never applied; a test verifies the model update uses the real weights only), per-client weight CV, per-round entropy.
* 6-episode probe (untrained, seed 0): 16 rounds, **44% single-update** (weight 1, nothing can matter), 50% two-client, 6% three-client; relevance M mean 0.005, max 0.139, **0 in all multi-update rounds -> relevance factor equals its floor eps_M = 0.1 for everyone; counterfactual weight change 0.**
* Goal-seeking reference policy (no learning, 3 scenarios): M median 0.54 (5-95%: 0.47-0.63), within-round factor spread median 1.08 (max/min), weight TV vs uniform median **1.5%** (5-95%: 0.6-3.4%).
**Answer:** with these symmetric AUVs the relevance factor has essentially no dynamic range: inert with untrained policies, <2% weight shift with a competent one. A measurable contribution of semantic weighting is **not expected** in the current setting; an ablation showing no difference would be the likely outcome, and that would be a valid finding, not a bug. Late-training behaviour of learned policies is unmeasured. Options (normalise M within a round, larger eta, lower eps_M, heterogeneous clients) are listed in D6 and not implemented.

## 5. Baseline fairness
Numerically equal across all 11 methods: observations, action constraints, environment, step budget, evaluation scenarios, seeds. **The 11 methods are not equivalent.** Declared non-equivalences N1-N10 are preserved. New: every method carries a deployment label (federated = ONE global actor; others own/shared actors), the comparability matrix is in the report (only 10 of 55 pairs, federated vs federated, are like-for-like), every comparison row carries its inherited non-equivalences (`evaluation/comparability.py`), and `run_all_experiments.py` stores the other deployment (`results_local_actors.json`) separately and writes `headline_policy.json`. Implication: federated-vs-non-federated differences mix the effect of federation with deployment (global vs per-AUV actors) and model-traffic cost; MAPPO differs in update budget (~31x fewer minibatches), policy class and sharing; greedy/Hungarian differ in allocator; independent TD3 and MADDPG are weaker by construction.

## 7. Pilot scope (for approval)
`python scripts/run_pilot_2seeds.py` (dry run) / `--approved`: 11 methods x seeds 0,1 x 5 episodes x 5 evaluation scenarios; predicted ~27 min sequential (extrapolation from the 12 s/episode measurement). ## 8. Remaining risks
R1 relevance has little dynamic range (above). R2 44% single-update rounds make staleness/relevance inert there; a lone stale update is applied at full strength. R3 no evidence any learner improves at 500 episodes; nothing comparative has been run. R4 10 seeds make Holm-Wilcoxon attainable, not likely; power unknown. R5 cost ~180-190 h sequential, other methods untimed. R6 retry parameters are a-priori design choices pending approval. R7 `clip_norm = None` would accept a huge finite update. R8 critics are not communication-constrained; MAPPO and allocator non-equivalences. R9 no DP, privacy or secure-aggregation claim. R10 the energy figure rests on an uncalibrated 30 W transmit-power assumption.
